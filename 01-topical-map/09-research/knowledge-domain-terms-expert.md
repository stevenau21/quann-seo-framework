## Query Vocabulary vs Document Vocabulary

| Layer | What It Is | Example (Texas Real Estate) |
|---|---|---|
| **Query Vocabulary** | What people type into search boxes | "how to buy a house in Texas," "Katy homes for sale," "first time buyer help" |
| **Document Vocabulary** | What experts actually use | "TREC promulgated contract," "homestead portability," "PITI calculation," "MUD PID structure" |

A page using only query vocabulary reads as AI-generated summary of common knowledge. A page using document vocabulary reads as created by someone who ACTUALLY DOES THIS for a living.

## How to Use These Terms

### Per-Page Density Rule

| Page Type | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 | Tier 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pillar page (e.g., Buying Guide) | 8-10 terms | 5-8 terms | 2-3 terms | 3-4 terms |
| Spoke page (e.g., FTHB Programs) | 5-7 terms | 3-5 terms | 1-2 terms | 2-3 terms |
| About page | 3-5 terms | 2-3 terms | 0-1 terms | 4-5 terms (ALL Tier 4) |
| Area guide | 3-5 terms | 5-8 terms | 2-3 terms | 1-2 terms |

### Integration Rule

Terms must be used **naturally, in context, with plain-English explanation on first use.** Never create a glossary page and dump all terms — that defeats the purpose. The terms must be woven into declarative sentences:

✅ "Texas uses metes and bounds legal descriptions, which means your property line is defined by physical landmarks and measurements rather than a plat map reference — important to understand before reviewing your survey."

❌ "Glossary: Metes and bounds — a property description system."

### What These Terms Prove

Per methodology: Google's NLP and entity extraction systems recognize these terms as domain-validated vocabulary. A page dense with Tier 2 and Tier 3 terms signals:
- **Human effort** — an AI trained on common web text does not naturally use these terms
- **Practitioner knowledge** — only someone who does Texas real estate transactions knows these
- **Differentiation** — competitors' blogs don't use these terms, so Quan's pages are algorithmically distinct
