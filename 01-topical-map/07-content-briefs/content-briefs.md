## Sentence Structure Rules (per SEO methodology)

### What Google NLP Prefers

| ✅ DO | ❌ AVOID |
|---|---|
| "The median home price in Katy is $385,000." | "You might find that Katy homes cost around $385k." |
| "FHA loans require a 3.5% minimum down payment." | "One option to consider could be an FHA loan." |
| "Katy ISD is rated A by the Texas Education Agency." | "Katy schools are generally considered pretty good." |
| "Closing costs in Texas average 2-5% of the purchase price." | "Closing costs can vary but they're usually in a certain range." |

### Entity-First Writing
- Lead with the entity (noun), not a fluffy intro
- Every paragraph anchors to a specific entity
- No "in this article we will discuss..." — state the fact

## Information Gain Auditing (Fact-per-Token)

Search engines reward **Unique Information Gain** — not just "unique content," but information your competitors *do not possess*. If every fact on your page already exists in the top 3 ranking results, the engine has no reason to rank you above established incumbents. You become redundant. Redundant content is filtered.

### The Fact-per-Token Requirement

Every spoke page must contain at least **three Unique Facts or Rare Attributes** not present in the current top 3 ranking results for the target query. These are facts only Quan — with his specific market knowledge, builder relationships, and transaction history — could provide.

### Unique Fact Types

| Fact Type | Definition | Example for Quan |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporal/Volatility Data** | Time-bounded statistics competitors can't replicate without the same data pipeline | "Texas sub-market volatility data Q1 2026" — Quan's internal MLS/historical data |
| **Process/Micro-Optimization** | A step or tactic the market doesn't publicly document | "How to stack a builder's quarter-end incentive with a lender credit AND a rate buy-down — the exact sequence" |
| **Entity Attribute** | A verifiable attribute of Quan that competitors don't possess | "License #0774451 — served X transactions in Katy zip codes 77494, 77450" |
| **Proprietary Framework** | A methodology or mental model Quan invented (and can name) | "The Bridge The Gap Method" — Quan's documented approach to buyer education |
| **Aggregated Pattern** | A trend Quan observed across his own transactions that isn't in public data | "In Q4 2025, 60% of Quan's Katy buyers used builder incentives worth $8K+" |
| **Missing Competitor Coverage** | A topic competitors literally don't address | "MUD vs PID tax structures explained in plain English with actual Katy examples" |

### The Redundancy Filter Test

Before publishing, ask for each page:

> "If I delete this page from the internet, does the world lose any information it can't get from the top 3 results?"

If the answer is "no" — the page is redundant. Do not publish. Add more Unique Facts.

### Information Density vs Information Gain

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Information Density** | Facts per token on the page | High (see Declarative Sentence Bank) |
| **Information Gain** | How many of those facts are NEW to the SERP | At least 3 unique facts per page |
| **Information Responsiveness** | How efficiently the page delivers its unique facts | Cost of Retrieval = Low (see cost-of-retrieval.md) |

All three must be high. A dense page full of already-known facts is redundant. A unique page that buries its facts in fluff has low Information Responsiveness. A responsive page without unique facts has no Information Gain.
