## 3. FAQ Section (For FAQPage Schema)

**Q1: How is buying a home in Texas different from other states?**
**Answer:** Texas is a non-disclosure state, meaning actual sale prices are not public record, making a local expert’s insight into "comparables" essential. Additionally, Texas has no state income tax, but this is offset by higher-than-average property taxes. The process also involves specific Texas-mandated disclosures, such as the Seller’s Disclosure Notice, which provides a detailed history of the property’s condition. Out-of-state buyers must also be aware that Texas homes often feature unique construction styles—like slab foundations—that require specific inspection expertise.

**Q2: What are closing costs for an out-of-state buyer in Katy?**
**Answer:** Closing costs in Katy typically range from 2% to 5% of the purchase price. These include loan origination fees, title insurance (which is often a point of negotiation between buyer and seller in Texas), appraisal fees, and recording fees. For relocating buyers, there may be additional costs associated with remote notarizations or courier services for original documents. It is critical to discuss "Seller Concessions" with your agent, as it is common in some Katy market cycles for sellers to contribute toward the buyer's closing costs to facilitate a quicker sale.

**Q3: Can I get down payment assistance as a relocating buyer?**
**Answer:** Yes, depending on your income level and the specific program. While many state-level programs (like TSHCP) prioritize Texas residents, there are federal grants and specific lender-sponsored programs available to first-time home buyers moving to the area. Some employers relocating staff to the Houston/Katy energy or medical corridors also provide relocation packages that can be applied toward a down payment. We recommend consulting with a Texas-based mortgage lender who specializes in relocation to identify all eligible grants or low-down-payment loan options.
