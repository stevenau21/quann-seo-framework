So, what is the difference between a “Happy Belated Birthday” and a “Belated Happy Birthday”? The first expression is semantically incorrect, while the latter is semantically correct. The key word here is “belated.” What does “belated” refer to in the phrase? The word “belated” modifies and refers to the words “Happy Birthday.” However, the placement of “belated” matters. “Belated” placed before “birthday” means that the birthday is late. “Belated” placed before “happy” means that the greeting is late. The first one is semantically incorrect because there is no such thing as a late birth date, while the latter is semantically correct. It is correct mainly because the greetings are allowed to be late when they are said after the actual birth date. Nonetheless, there are still many people who use “Happy Belated Birthday” despite being semantically incorrect. What does Belated Mean?
Belated is defined as “coming later than expected.” The question is, can you use belated for punctuality? No, it is not allowed to be used for punctuality. Belated implies something being late. The meaning of the word “belated” is “coming later than expected or delayed beyond the usual.” The word belated is not suited to be used when talking about punctuality. Belated is the opposite of being punctual. For instance, the gifts received after the day of a birthday are belated birthday gifts. The etymology of the word belated was the verb “belate,” meaning “delay” or “be late.” The first known use of the word “belated” was in 1670. The word belated tended to mostly turn up in the form of its past participle, “belated from the beginning.” Belate fell out of use, leaving the word belated as an adjective that holds the same notion of delay. The words “belate” and “belated” were derived from the adjective “late.” The word belate was formed by adding the prefix be-, meaning“to cause to be” with late. Belated once meant “overtaken by night,” which was the first meaning of the adjective, but it has fallen into disuse.
